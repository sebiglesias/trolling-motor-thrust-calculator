Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 2 lb per 100 lb rule for trolling motor thrust?
The general marine rule of thumb is that you need at least **2 pounds of thrust for every 100 pounds of total loaded boat weight**. When calculating weight, always include the dry boat hull, outboard motor, full fuel tank, trolling motor batteries, and all passengers and gear on board.
How do you measure for the correct trolling motor shaft length?
Measure vertically from the top of the bow deck down to the waterline. **Add at least 16 to 20 inches** to this measurement. The propeller needs to be submerged at least 12 inches below the surface so it doesn't cavitate or suck air in windy chop. If you fish in rough open water or ocean waves, add 24 inches.
Why is a 24V or 36V trolling motor system superior to 12V?
12-volt trolling motors are limited to roughly 55 lbs of thrust because pulling more thrust would exceed 60 Amps, overheating cables. Stepping up to 24V (80 lbs thrust) or 36V (112 lbs thrust) delivers massive holding power in stiff wind while drawing lower amperage, extending battery run time by up to 50%.
